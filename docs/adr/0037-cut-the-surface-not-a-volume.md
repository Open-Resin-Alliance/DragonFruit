# ADR-0037: Cut the contour by splitting the surface, not by differencing a wafer out of the volume

**Status**: accepted

Context: the contour cut used to span the user's seam with a soap-film membrane, thicken it into a 0.1 mm wafer, difference that out of the model, and then reconstruct which of the resulting components was the freed piece. Over one session on a single 500k-triangle model it failed in six visibly different ways, and all six came from one decision: **the cutter is a finite wafer whose rim must end exactly on the skin, and the split is reconstructed afterwards by guessing.** Every fix was scaffolding under one half of that or the other. The measurements are in [the redesign report](../dev/organic-cut-surface-redesign.md).

Decision: treat the split as a **surface** operation. The seam is a curve lying on the skin, so cut the skin along it until the seam IS mesh edges, flood-fill each side over the face graph, and close each piece with the same membrane sewn to the cut's own edge chain — reversed on the far side, so the two halves share their cut face and mate exactly. No cutter, no kerf, no epsilon, nothing to classify. The wafer stays **behind** it rather than being deleted: a flood fill leaks through a hole in the skin and the boolean forgives that, so meshes with torn skin still need it. A flat plane cut remains a separate mode and never a fallback — a plane fitted to the same loop is infinite and would slice clean across the body.

Consequences: **cut thickness stops being a setting.** It was never the fix (the same seam fails identically at 0.1, 0.3 and 0.5 mm) and it was quietly doing a second job, since the slab's thickness at the rim doubled as sideways reach across a fold. The wafer now picks its own sub-resolution slab, and the number the user sets means **joint clearance** — the gap a glued joint needs if the assembled model is not to come out fatter than it started, which artists ask for as part of cutting for print. That gap cannot be measured out after the fact, because the two halves share their cut face and there is nothing between them to widen; it is cut, by splitting along the seam moved both ways, half the clearance each, and discarding the strip of skin between. Because it moves the CURVE and lets the exact splitter do the rest, the gap resolves far below a triangle — dropping faces near the rim instead could not resolve a tenth of a millimetre on a mesh whose triangles are a third of one. It costs the joint nothing: the tenon is anchored on the cut face of the piece it stands on, so its base is inside its own material at any clearance, and it simply crosses the gap. Two checks are worth having whatever else changes, because they answer questions the boolean could not even ask: removing the seam's band and seeing whether the surface still holds together says whether a cut can possibly work at all — a loop around a handle separates nothing, so no cutter of any shape can free it — and every piece is checked for closing into a solid before it is handed back, so a cut that cannot be made cleanly refuses out loud instead of returning something unsliceable.
