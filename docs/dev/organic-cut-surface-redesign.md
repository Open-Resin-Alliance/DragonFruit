# The organic contour cut: why the wafer kept failing, and what the surface redesign measured

Working notes behind [ADR-0037](../adr/0037-cut-the-surface-not-a-volume.md). Kept because
almost every paragraph is a measurement that closes off a road, and the roads are tempting
enough to be walked twice. The ADR records the decision; this records the evidence.

Context: the contour cut spans a user-drawn surface seam with a soap-film membrane, thickens it into a razor-thin wafer (0.1 mm), differences that wafer out of the model with `manifold-csg`, and then reconstructs which of the resulting connected components is the freed piece. Over one session against a single 500k-triangle model (a tentacled tower, 182 × 90 × 264 mm, covered in shingles and rock folds), that pipeline failed in six visibly different ways: a cut that "succeeded" and handed back a 548-triangle flake from the far side of the model; a cut that severed nothing while reporting a clean cut face; a cut that handed back a 16-triangle crumb 0.4 mm across as a freed piece; a three-loop cut that returned the body plus two pieces nobody asked for; and two variants that refused with a message blaming the seam for not closing, which was the one thing that had gone right. Each looked like its own bug. They were not.

The first thing that changed the investigation was instrumentation, and that is the process lesson worth keeping: a cut that misbehaves on a 500k-triangle mesh cannot be reasoned about from a screenshot, and the user cannot reproduce an experiment (every recompile drops them out of `tauri:dev`, and the VOXL format does not persist cut structures, so each attempt is similar but never identical). `DF_CUT_DUMP` writes the exact inputs of every cut — staged mesh plus the options, seam included — and `cut_replay` runs them again outside the app; `DF_CUT_DEBUG` traces the shells the model had before the boolean, every island's size, position, distance to the cut face and which side it fell on, how much of the rim was still buried in material, and where the two sides of the cut still hold on to each other. Twenty-odd captures later, every claim below is a measurement rather than a hypothesis, and a `cut_bench` harness replays all of them against any new idea in about a minute.

What the measurements found, in the order they fell:

**Shells the model already carried were being handed over as freed pieces.** `decompose` returns every connected component of the boolean's result, including the ones the input had. The user's STL ships a 548-triangle flake 108 mm away from any seam; being neither the largest component nor attached to anything, it passed for "the piece that came free". Fixed by requiring a freed island to sit ON a cut face (a band of a few kerf thicknesses), with orphan shells riding along with the body.

**Sides were being read from island centroids.** `signed_side_distance` signs a point against the nearest membrane triangle, which is meaningful for a point on the cut face and meaningless for one a hundred millimetres away: the freed spire came out at +1.03 and the entire body at +11.2 — the same side. Fixed by letting each island vote with the vertices it has on the cut face.

**The wafer's rim has to land exactly on the skin, and mostly does not.** Seam smoothing cuts the corner at every wiggle and sinks the line up to 0.14 mm below the surface, more than the fixed 0.1 mm widen; and a seam drawn under an overhanging detail is bridged by the lip passing over it, which the rim never touches. The rim now ray-casts its way out to the last skin it crosses, on a 1 mm leash, and stays put where the way out is longer than that (so the cut cannot creep outside the seam the user drew).

**A multi-loop cut sent bare waypoints as one of its seams.** Restoring the loops after a cut or an undo deliberately drops each cached geodesic, and only the ACTIVE loop's effect recomputes one, so in a three-loop cut the other loops travelled as four sparse waypoints. A membrane spanned over four waypoints is a flat quad tens of millimetres off the surface they were drawn on, and it cuts like one. Missing seams are now recomputed when the cut is applied, and the "waypoints can stand in for a seam" fallback is gone.

**Kerf crumbs counted as pieces, and the body was decomposed after grouping.** A 16-triangle chip 0.4 mm across came off a cut face exactly like a real piece; anything smaller across than ten kerfs now rides with the body. And the multi-loop path decomposed `part_a` again three lines after the grouping had tucked the loose shells into it, handing them straight back — which is why the 548-triangle flake kept reappearing even after it had been "fixed".

Underneath all six sits one decision: **the cutter is a finite wafer whose rim must end exactly on the skin, and the split is reconstructed afterwards by guessing.** Every fix above is scaffolding to prop up one half of that or the other. That is the pattern, and it is worth naming because it predicts where the next failure comes from.

Two things were measured and refuted, and are recorded here so they are not tried again. **Wafer thickness is not the fix**: on some seams 0.3–0.5 mm severs where 0.1 mm does not, which looks like a cure until you notice why — the slab's thickness at the rim doubles as sideways reach across a fold, by accident — and the user's own controlled run (same seam at 0.1, 0.3 and 0.5 mm) fails identically at all three. **The "crown" is not the fix either**: extending the membrane's rim outward until it clears the model, closing it into a half-space solid and calling `Manifold::split` produces two non-empty parts on 32 of 32 seams, which is meaningless — a half-space always divides a model. On the cuts that already work it splits the model in half (284 808 + 256 562 triangles where the correct answer is 20 778 + 521 698). Outward from a seam, along the surface, is not air: it is the rest of the model. Also measured and cleared of suspicion: membrane and slab self-intersections (zero everywhere), the polyline's starting index (rotating it changes nothing), and whether the seam leaves the skin (0.06–0.32 mm on every captured seam — within a triangle's thickness — except the four-waypoint bug above, at 6.25 mm).

Decision (proposed): treat the split as a **surface** operation rather than a volumetric boolean. The seam is a curve lying on the skin; which side of it a triangle is on is answerable by walking faces, with no epsilon, no cutter and no reconstruction. Mark the faces the seam runs through, split them exactly along the curve, flood-fill each side, and cap both with the membrane, which already shares the seam as its boundary — two watertight solids that mate exactly, with no kerf and nothing to classify. A probe of the crux (mark the band one face wide, remove it, see what the surface falls into) agrees with the boolean on every cut that works today, and separates the ones it cannot: 56 484 faces come free on the three-loop capture, 13 010–16 506 on the two-loop ones, in ~160 ms against 600–3000 ms for the boolean.

Consequences: the same probe also explains the failures the boolean could never explain, and the explanation is topological rather than numerical. **A single loop around a tentacle that leaves the body and fuses back to it cannot free anything, by any cutter, because such a curve does not separate the surface** — it encircles a handle. Every single-loop attempt on those tentacles was doomed before any geometry ran, and the app should say so instead of blaming the seam. That is a check worth having whatever happens to the redesign: removing the seam's band and finding the surface still in one piece is a cheap, exact answer to "can this cut possibly work", available before any cutting starts. The redesign's own risks are the exact splitting of the faces the seam crosses, keeping the seam on the surface through smoothing (measured fine today, but it is now load-bearing), and meshes with holes, where a flood fill leaks from one side to the other and the boolean is the more forgiving of the two — so the wafer path should stay as a fallback rather than being deleted. The captured dumps live outside the repo (`~/.claude/df-cut-dumps`), are throwaway rather than fixtures, and must never be committed as test data: the unit tests stay on synthetic geometry.

## What the redesign measured

Landed as `surface_split` (cut the skin along the seam) plus `surface_cap` (close each piece with the membrane as its lid), tried ahead of the wafer in `organic_cut_contour`. Against the same 32 captured seams: **the seam becomes mesh edges on 32 of 32 without adding a single edge that belongs to other than two faces** (the user's STL arrives with three, and leaves with three), and **29 of 32 close into solids** — the 21 seams that separate nothing correctly get no lid, the 8 that separate get one, and three refuse out loud (two where the rim pinches at a vertex four cut edges meet, one where the soap film's interior runs along an edge the skin already has). Those three fall back to the wafer, which is the whole reason it is still here.

One rule carries both halves, and getting it wrong cost a session: **an edge the rebuild adds — a seam chord, or the base of an ear — may never run along an edge of the face it is added to.** A fan from a fixed vertex breaks it constantly, because a face's boundary carries the crossings its NEIGHBOURS made on their shared edges, so three of its vertices are often in a straight line; the fan then emits a triangle spanning an edge the neighbour has already split, plus a needle of no area. Both faces either side do it at once, so the edge comes out on four triangles: closed, but not manifold, and the flood fill walks straight through what was meant to be a wall. That was the 8-of-32 leak this document originally recorded as an open gap and misattributed to the walk revisiting a face. It is asked structurally — does one edge of the face carry both ends? — never by measuring how straight three points are. **Also refuted, so it is not tried again**: dropping the needles by relative area (what is left leans on an edge that no longer exists, which is a hole), cutting a chord once per face, and dropping repeated triangles. All three treat the symptom.

The consequence for the UI is that **thickness stops being structural**. It was never the fix — the same seam fails identically at 0.1, 0.3 and 0.5 mm — and it was quietly doing a second job, since the slab's thickness at the rim doubled as sideways reach across a fold. The wafer now picks its own slab thickness (sub-resolution, its business alone), and the number the user sets became **joint clearance**, zero by default, spent on the mortise-and-tenon fit. With it went the tenon's `kerf_mm` and `sink_frame_into_part_a`, which existed only to reach across a gap that no longer exists.

## The joint clearance, measured

The clearance is a real gap, cut rather than measured out afterwards: the seam is offset to both sides by half of it (surface normal × seam tangent, each moved point put back on the skin), the surface is split along both curves, and the strip of skin between them is discarded. On `cut-0`, the material removed comes to **11.9 mm³ at 0.2 mm and 30.0 mm³ at 0.5 mm** — implying a cut face of 59.3 and 60.2 mm² by two independent routes, so the gap is linear in the setting and is the size asked for.

Two things are worth knowing. **Below about 0.1 mm the clearance is swallowed**: the walk snaps a crossing that lands within 2% of an edge onto the vertex, so two offsets a few hundredths apart collapse onto the same crossings and no strip forms at all. That is under print resolution, so it is a floor rather than a bug, but it is silent. And **the strip must be found structurally, not by distance** — a first attempt asked which piece stayed within a threshold of the drawn seam and failed at 0.1 mm, because a drawn seam is a couple of dozen points on a model 180 mm across and the nearest *point* can be millimetres from a strip a tenth of a millimetre wide. The strip is instead the piece that a seam's two caps BOTH face, which needs no threshold. Pairing caps to seams is measured the same way, since rims come out of the cut in the order the surface was walked, which is no order at all.

Refuted on the way: **eroding faces near the rim** instead of offsetting the curve. It cannot resolve the gap — at a tenth of a millimetre on a mesh whose triangles are a third of one, face-level removal is quantised to the triangle and takes out either nothing or far too much.
